Getting started

From download to your first shell.

Install the Mac companion with step 1. The remaining steps are for existing private iPhone testers: open each one as you connect your devices.

  • MacmacOS 15 or later, with bassh installed
  • iPhonebassh installed through the private TestFlight beta
  • TailscaleBoth devices on the same network

Keep your Mac awake and connected. You don’t need Xcode or Go.

  1. Install bassh for MacExtract the ZIP, move bassh to Applications, then open it.+
    1. Download the ZIP using the button above. Double-click it in Finder to extract bassh.app.
    2. Drag bassh.app into Applications.
    3. Open bassh from Applications.

    On the first open

    macOS may ask you to confirm opening an app downloaded from the internet. Choose Open. This signed, notarized release includes Apple’s notarization ticket and needs no security override.

    You may also see a permission prompt if you choose a folder. Keep Gatekeeper enabled.

  2. Connect with TailscalePut your Mac and iPhone on the same private network.+
    Tailscale for Mac ↗Tailscale for iPhone ↗
    1. Install Tailscale on both devices.
    2. Sign both into the same Tailscale network and leave Tailscale connected.
    3. Open bassh on your Mac and choose Add iPhone to start the walkthrough.
  3. Enable Remote LoginAllow SSH access for the Mac account you’ll use.+

    On your Mac, go to:

    System Settings → General → Sharing → Remote Login

    1. Open the Remote Login settings and turn it on.
    2. Under Allow access for, choose Only these users and add the Mac account you intend to use.

    The companion leaves this permission in your hands.

    Apple’s Remote Login guide ↗
  4. Authorize your iPhone’s keyGive this iPhone permission to connect to your Mac.+

    On your iPhone

    1. Open bassh → Set up with Tailscale.
    2. Create or choose an SSH key, then copy its public key to the Mac.

    On your Mac

    1. Paste the public key into Authorize iPhone key in the walkthrough.
    2. Compare the key fingerprint, check the displayed Mac account, then authorize it.

    Copy only the public key. Your private key stays on the iPhone.

  5. Open your first shellSave the connection and verify your Mac’s identity.+

    Save your Mac in the iPhone app with these details:

    Address
    Your Mac’s Tailscale address
    Username
    The short username shown in the Mac guide
    SSH port
    22
    SSH key
    The key you just authorized

    On the first connection, compare the full SSH host fingerprint on your iPhone with the matching fingerprint in the Mac guide. Approve it only if they match, then open the terminal.

A few things to know

What’s included in the Mac companion?

The guided Tailscale and macOS Remote Login setup. The Mac download is signed and notarized; the iPhone beta remains private. Tailscale is installed separately; advanced QR pairing is outside this preview. No public router port forwarding is needed, and your Tailscale network must allow the iPhone to reach the Mac’s SSH port.

How do I update bassh for Mac?

Download a newer version from this page and replace bassh.app in Applications. This preview has no automatic updater. Your Mac companion identity and saved pairing data use the existing storage locations.

How do I remove SSH access?

Remove the exact iPhone public-key line from your Mac’s ~/.ssh/authorized_keys to block new SSH logins, and close any existing sessions. You can also turn off Remote Login if you no longer need it. Removing the app or a saved Mac on iPhone does not revoke SSH access. The companion’s QR pairing controls do not manage macOS Remote Login.
