Getting started

Connect your own Mac.

You’ll need the Mac preview, the bassh iPhone beta and Tailscale on both devices. Xcode and Go are not required.

  1. Install the Mac preview

    Open the DMG, drag bassh.app into Applications, then open it from there.

    If macOS says it cannot verify the developer or check the app, and you trust this download, open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway after attempting to launch it. Confirm the per-app exception. Keep Gatekeeper enabled.

    Apple’s instructions for opening an unnotarized app ↗

  2. Connect both devices to Tailscale

    Install the official Tailscale Mac app and iPhone app. Sign your Mac and iPhone into the same Tailscale network and keep it connected. In the Mac companion, choose Add iPhone to open the walkthrough.

  3. Enable Remote Login for your account

    On the Mac, open System Settings → General → Sharing → Remote Login. Enable it and allow only the Mac account you intend to use. The companion does not switch this on automatically.

    Apple’s Remote Login guide ↗

  4. Authorize your iPhone’s public key

    On iPhone, open bassh → Set up with Tailscale, then create or choose an SSH key. Copy its public key to the Mac.

    Paste it into Authorize iPhone key in the Mac walkthrough. Compare the key fingerprint and explicitly authorize it for the displayed Mac account. Your private key stays on the iPhone.

  5. Save your Mac and open a shell

    Use the Mac’s Tailscale address, the short username shown in the Mac guide and SSH port 22. Choose the SSH key you authorized.

    Before trusting the first connection, compare the full SSH host fingerprint shown on iPhone with the matching fingerprint in the Mac guide. Approve a match, then open the terminal. Keep the Mac awake and connected while you use it.

A few things to know

What’s included in this preview?

The guided Tailscale and macOS Remote Login setup. Tailscale is installed separately; advanced QR pairing is outside this preview. No public router port forwarding is needed, and your Tailscale network must allow the iPhone to reach the Mac’s SSH port.

How do I update bassh for Mac?

Download a newer version from this page and replace bassh.app in Applications. This preview has no automatic updater. Your Mac companion identity and saved pairing data use the existing storage locations.

How do I remove SSH access?

Remove the exact iPhone public-key line from your Mac’s ~/.ssh/authorized_keys to block new SSH logins, and close any existing sessions. You can also turn off Remote Login if you no longer need it. Removing the app or a saved Mac on iPhone does not revoke SSH access. The companion’s QR pairing controls do not manage macOS Remote Login.
